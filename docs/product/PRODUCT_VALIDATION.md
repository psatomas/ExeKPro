# Product validation

## Assumptions to test

- Developers experience meaningful friction integrating constrained, observable on-chain operations.
- A customer-controlled authorization and verifiable outcome are more valuable than a generic transaction wrapper.
- Teams will value a managed monitoring/configuration layer after the local verified-execution workflow is credible.
- Buyers will pay for reduced integration and operational burden without giving up transaction authority.

## Risks and questions

Competitive products may already cover routing, transaction automation, monitoring, or policy enforcement better within a narrower category. Validate whether integration pain is authorization, observability, route control, incident response, or something else before expanding scope.

Ask developers:

1. Which operation is currently hardest to authorize, constrain, and audit?
2. Who must approve it, and how do they recover from a bad or duplicate request?
3. What evidence is required to call the operation settled?
4. Would a local SDK-first workflow solve an immediate problem? What prevents adoption?
5. Which controls must remain on-chain and customer-owned?

Ask economic buyers: what current build/operate cost exists; which measurable risk or time saving matters; who owns budget; and what pricing basis (projects, executions, monitoring) would be credible only after value is proven.

## Evidence required

Record interview count, customer segment, workflow, stated pain, current workaround, integration time estimate, objection, and follow-up evidence. Do not convert interview claims into validated market facts without repeatable evidence. Product validation runs in parallel with [the roadmap](../planning/ROADMAP.md).
